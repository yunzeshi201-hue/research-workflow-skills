# Use the evidence library in scientific writing

Load the relevant external catalog and paper notes before drafting the literature-dependent claims. Search by research question, sample/material, conditions, and proposed mechanism, not only a keyword or a similar numerical value. Reopen the original source for decisive claims, contradictory records, or notes with limited access. A recent note date does not certify a source, settle a conflict, or eliminate the need to verify a new claim.

For a full manuscript, read the available `sci-brain:paper-writer` skill and use its figure-led planning, venue guidance, narrative organization, and quality checks. Figure-level observations, provisional captions, and requested evidence-supported paragraphs remain in this skill's immediate drafting route. The user's current writing request and known preferences take precedence over a supporting skill's default planning checkpoints: do not re-request permission to draft, require a venue before writing supported text, or require a separate narrative approval when the user has authorized proceeding. Ask a substantive question only when its answer materially changes the draft, and keep independent work moving. For a requested manuscript critique, read `sci-brain:paper-reviewer` and use its review/edit workflow. A request to learn from published papers remains a reading task and does not require an editable manuscript.

Preserve source units and normalization, including `a.u.`. Missing calibration is a stated limit, not permission to invent a physical unit or change scientific values to satisfy a formatting preference. Missing factual evidence may limit a particular claim; it does not block unrelated supported drafting.

## Match each claim to the evidence

Use the claim ledger in [evidence-records.md](evidence-records.md) for consequential claims, especially numerical comparisons and mechanism assignments. Resolve citation keys through the existing bibliography/metadata workflow; never construct bibliographic facts from memory. Cite primary studies for primary evidence and distinguish reviews used for orientation.

Before promoting a statement into manuscript prose, check:

- The source supports the actual statement, under the stated material, sample, conditions, and time regime.
- Reported observation, authors' interpretation, and the present manuscript's inference remain distinguishable.
- Values use the same definition and units; a probe wavelength is not recast as a fitted peak center, and a normalized response is not silently compared as an absolute yield.
- The inference strength matches its controls. A plausible model may be “consistent with” a result; stronger causal language needs evidence that discriminates important alternatives.
- Conflicting primary evidence and transfer limits are addressed when they could change the conclusion. An unresolved record is not used as a numerical ground truth.

For a missing source, retrieve it or narrow/qualify the claim. If the claim is central and remains unsupported, mark it as needing evidence and continue unrelated drafting. Do not hide the gap behind a generic citation or invent an experimental result. Source-limited findings may be discussed as such when useful, but must not be presented as fully verified.

## Evidence-based critique

Anchor criticism to a specific claim, figure, method, or omitted control and explain its scientific consequence. Distinguish a demonstrated error from an ambiguity and from a useful additional experiment. Do not infer author intent, competence, or misconduct from an unexplained spectrum or incomplete report. Propose the smallest discriminating check that would resolve the uncertainty.

Before delivery, check the main claims against the ledger and confirm citation resolution. Report remaining evidence limitations along with format/compile validation from the writing workflow. Do not claim to have read inaccessible SI or to have validated a mechanism solely from a paper's abstract.
