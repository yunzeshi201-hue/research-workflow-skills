# Targeted Research Learning Plan

Use this structure to connect preparation to active research.
Replace instructional sentences with project-specific content when producing the plan.
Do not fill every category merely to make the document appear complete.

## Research task and starting evidence

State the next research decision or task in concrete terms.
Explain what the researcher must be able to do to carry it out responsibly.
Summarize demonstrated capabilities, known gaps, and assumptions about background or resources.
If the research direction is provisional, distinguish the learning target from a claim of scientific novelty.

## Diagnostic task

First include a brief orientation to the concepts needed now, with verified references and inspected locations. Match its depth to prior evidence of understanding. Record whether an applied check was answered, satisfied by existing work, skipped at the user's request, or remains pending; these states have different implications.

Describe a short task that resembles the intended research work.
Record what was attempted, what was observed, and which specific gap the result reveals.
Use existing work where it provides equivalent evidence.
If the diagnostic is pending, state which part of the plan depends on its outcome.

## Prerequisite graph

Use a dependency list or a small diagram.
Each link must explain why the upstream capability is necessary for the downstream task.
Avoid long lists of topics without a relationship to the research decision.

For each capability, record:

- **Capability:** an action the researcher should be able to perform.
- **Research use:** the decision, inference, or method choice it supports.
- **Dependencies:** capabilities that must come first.
- **Priority:** needed before this step, learn alongside the work, or defer.
- **Evidence of the gap:** diagnostic result, supplied context, or explicit assumption.

## Learning blocks

Create a short block for each priority capability:

- **Learning objective:** the concrete change in what the researcher can do.
- **Core idea:** the concept or method and its key assumptions.
- **Resource:** a verified source with the relevant chapter, section, figure, or other locator.
- **Practice:** a worked calculation, interpretation, reconstruction, critique, or small implementation.
- **Readiness check:** a fresh task and the observable criterion for completing it.
- **If the check fails:** the specific prerequisite to revisit or the support to add.
- **Research contribution:** an artifact or decision this learning block makes possible now.

Choose the amount of study from the task, not from a fixed number of papers or study weeks.
Where a time estimate is useful, label it as a planning estimate and state its assumptions.

## Readiness evidence

Track capability, check performed, result, remaining limitation, and the next action.
Use statuses such as demonstrated, partial, not yet checked, or needs support.
Do not reduce understanding to a percentage without a defined assessment basis.
Record successful reasoning as well as the answer.
For implementation tasks, record the independent check rather than treating a successful program run as sufficient.
For measurement tasks, distinguish conceptual readiness from supervised practical competence when applicable.

## Research in parallel

Specify one bounded research task that can begin with the current capabilities.
Name its inputs, expected output, assumptions, and checks.
Explain what new information it can provide and what it cannot establish.
Label exercises, illustrative data, and toy models clearly.
Identify any part that must wait for a prerequisite and why.

## Source record

For each important resource, record its verified citation or direct URL, locator, access status, and role in the plan.
Access status should distinguish full text, partial text, abstract only, and metadata only.
For research-specific claims, distinguish reported observations, authors' interpretations, and your own inferences.
If the plan relies on current field status, record the search date, query scope, and literature cutoff.
Identify unavailable sources and provide a supported alternative when possible.

## Next decision and revision rule

State the immediate learning action and the immediate research action.
Define the readiness evidence required to proceed to the next step.
Explain which observation would cause the learning plan to shrink, expand, or change direction.
End preparation for this step when the required capability is demonstrated with its limitations understood.
