# Research Foundations

This is a helper, not an additional workflow stage. When attached to `research-guide`, inspect the project workflow record and stay within the current stage. Learning exercises do not accept a subfield, select an accepted question set, authorize website construction, or unlock review drafting. A standalone learning request can be answered directly without starting a four-stage project.

Teach enough of the right foundations to make and test a research decision.
The output is a dependency-aware learning plan with observable readiness checks and an immediate research task.
Do not turn preparation into an open-ended course or assume that reading a paper establishes understanding.

## Start from the intended work

Use the current field, question, background, and resources already available.
If a research question exists, begin with the decision or inference the researcher must make next.
If only a field is supplied, choose a provisional representative task and label it as a learning target, not a novel project.
Ask a focused question only when the missing background would materially change the plan.
Do not require a complete biography or a fixed curriculum before helping.

Identify the work the researcher should be able to perform:

- Explain a phenomenon or competing model in operational terms.
- Derive or estimate the quantity needed to choose a method.
- Interpret a measurement, dataset, argument, or computational result.
- Detect an invalid assumption, artifact, confound, or evaluation failure.
- Design the next informative comparison or check.

Select only the capabilities relevant to the project.

## Diagnose knowledge with a useful task

Start with a concise orientation to the concepts needed for the current task, usually three to five key ideas rather than a field-wide lecture. Attach verified references with the relevant chapter, section, figure, or page and explain what each teaches. Use authoritative teaching sources for established fundamentals and primary papers for specific findings. If access is unavailable, mark the explanation and reading suggestions as provisional; do not invent bibliographic details or pretend to have inspected a source.

Then, where background remains uncertain, offer one to three short applied questions matched to the user's goal. Let the researcher explain a prediction, interpret evidence, or identify a limiting assumption. Use a fresh example rather than asking them to repeat the preceding explanation. These counts are defaults, not a mandatory test. Reuse demonstrated competence from earlier answers or supplied work, honor a request to skip, and keep a paper-only task moving without a quiz.

Respond to the actual answer before assigning a remedial path. State what is demonstrated, what remains uncertain, and the smallest useful next step. Avoid global labels such as beginner or expert and arbitrary grades. If the user does not answer, keep the depth provisional rather than treating silence as success or failure.

Prefer a small authentic task over self-ratings or a quiz of definitions.
Ask for a prediction, calculation, interpretation, reconstruction, or critique appropriate to the intended work.
Inspect existing work when available so the researcher need not repeat demonstrated skills.
Treat an incorrect answer as evidence about a specific dependency, not a global lack of ability.
Separate conceptual gaps from notation, implementation, measurement, and communication difficulties.
When no diagnostic can be performed, make the plan provisional and state what the first task will reveal.

## Build the prerequisite graph

Work backward from the research task to the capabilities it depends on.
For each dependency, explain why it is needed and what decision would be unreliable without it.
Order dependencies by what must be learned first; allow independent branches to proceed together.
Use only the relevant parts of concept knowledge, theory, measurement, statistics, computation, and research practice.
Do not impose every category on every discipline.

Classify each learning item as:

- **Needed before this step:** a missing capability that would invalidate the immediate work.
- **Learn alongside the work:** a capability that can grow through a bounded task with checks.
- **Defer:** useful background that does not change the current decision.

Prefer transferable concepts and worked examples to memorizing isolated paper-specific values.
Teach the model's assumptions, limits, and relation to observables along with its equations or terminology.
For measurement work, connect the physical or operational quantity to how the instrument or procedure produces data.
For computational work, connect implementation checks to the scientific claim and its evaluation conditions.
For statistical work, connect uncertainty, sampling, dependence, and identifiability to the intended inference.

## Attach sources and readiness checks

Select a small set of resources that directly support the required capabilities.
Use authoritative teaching material for foundations and inspected primary studies for research-specific claims.
Verify a source's identity, relevant section, and access before recommending it as a precise learning resource.
Check current primary sources when the plan depends on a claimed unresolved problem or a changing method.
Record source locators and access status; do not imply a full reading from a search result or abstract.
Separate the source's result from the authors' interpretation and from your proposed learning exercise.

Every important learning item needs an observable check.
State the task, the evidence of readiness, and the consequence of a failed check.
Examples of checks include reproducing a simple result, deriving a limiting case, predicting a control outcome, or finding a confound in an argument.
Choose checks that reveal understanding rather than merely matching wording or reproducing copied code.
Where practical, use a fresh example after teaching so success is not just recall of the answer.
Treat an unassisted explanation as one useful check, not proof of experimental or computational competence.
Do not invent pass thresholds; justify numerical tolerances from the task or use a qualitative criterion with explicit reasons.

## Learn while doing useful research

Pair each priority learning block with a bounded contribution to the project.
Useful contributions can include an evidence comparison, reproducibility check, toy model, feasibility estimate, or annotated protocol.
Label toy calculations and exercises so they are not mistaken for new scientific evidence.
Separate learning a known result from investigating a genuinely unresolved question.
Revise the plan when the task reveals a different bottleneck or an unnecessary prerequisite.
Stop adding preparation once the researcher can perform the next step with appropriate checks.

Use [learning-plan.md](references/learning-plan.md) for a reusable plan and progress record.
End with what to learn now, what to do now, the readiness check, and the next research decision.
Keep personal background, completed exercises, and project evidence in project files rather than this installed skill.
If available in the skill catalog, `research-guide`, `research-question`, or `research-test-design` can support the next stage.
Use these optional companions only within the active stage and its accepted prerequisites. Return the demonstrated capability and remaining gap to that stage's checkpoint. Further study or experimental execution remains a proposed next action unless separately requested within the authorized scope.
