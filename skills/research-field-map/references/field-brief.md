# Field Brief

Use this structure for a decision document, not a bibliography dump.
Replace instructional sentences with project-specific content when producing the brief.
Omit sections that do not affect the decision, but retain evidence limits and the next action.

## Decision and scope

Begin from the supplied account of what was done, what was observed, and what is wanted next. Record evidence status, current interpretations, resources, and the decision to be changed. An already specific research direction can be the proposed scope; a new chat does not require a new field search. Do not count the intake as a fifth workflow stage.

State the field, the choice being made, the desired contribution, and the planning horizon.
Summarize known resources and constraints. Mark missing constraints as assumptions.
State the literature cutoff and the boundary of this assessment.

## Working field map

Use a table with these columns:

| Subfield or problem family | Central question | Established result | Remaining limitation | Evidence used | Source IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Populate rows only for branches relevant to the choice.
Explain connections between complementary branches instead of forcing a single ranking.

## Candidate directions

Create one short card per serious candidate with these fields:

- **Question:** the uncertainty the direction addresses.
- **Status:** learning gap, practical bottleneck, documented disagreement, or proposed open question.
- **Nearest work:** the closest inspected study and the precise remaining difference.
- **Why it matters:** the understanding or capability an answer would change.
- **Possible contribution:** the result this project could reasonably aim to establish.
- **Alternative explanation:** why the apparent opportunity might be less useful than it seems.
- **Resources and dependencies:** what is available, required, or uncertain.
- **First informative check:** one action that could confirm feasibility or remove a weak candidate.
- **Decision after the check:** what would justify continuing, revising, or dropping this direction.

An inferred gap must be labeled as an inference, with the supporting evidence and remaining search limits.

## Comparison and recommendation

Use a comparison table with importance, uncertainty, tractability, resource fit, and information value as separate columns.
Give reasons rather than unexplained numbers.
Include confidence in the assessment and the evidence that could overturn it.
Identify the preferred direction under the stated constraints and a useful alternative if a key resource is unavailable.
Explain the relationship between the first project and the broader scientific goal.

## Learning needed to decide

Identify only the concepts or methods whose absence currently prevents a sound choice.
For each, name a concrete capability to demonstrate and a short resource or exercise that develops it.
Separate knowledge needed before the first action from knowledge that can be learned during the project.

## Evidence ledger

Use one record per important source:

- **Source ID and verified citation:** title, year, persistent identifier or direct URL.
- **Claim supported:** the exact field-map statement this source informs.
- **Locator:** section, figure, table, page, theorem, or other stable location.
- **Access:** full text, partial text, abstract only, or metadata only.
- **Evidence type:** reported observation, authors' interpretation, or analyst inference.
- **Scope and limits:** conditions under which the result applies and what it cannot establish.

Keep source-level limitations attached to the recommendation they affect.

## Search record and unresolved coverage

Record the search date, databases or source sites, queries, and coverage cutoff.
List terminology variants and the nearest-work or contrary-evidence searches performed.
State major inaccessible sources and coverage gaps without presenting the search as exhaustive.
Distinguish "no matching result found" from "the field has not answered this question."

## Stage-1 checkpoint

Include the brief revision, selected subfield, explicit boundaries and exclusions, essential assumptions, and unresolved issues. Provide a short list of verified foundational or representative experimental papers, their roles, relevant sections, and why they help establish the scope. Do not imply that illustrative questions are the accepted stage-2 question set.

Present the brief to the user and ask whether this scope is settled for question selection. Record `ready_for_confirmation` until actual acceptance is established. Preserve accepted versions and the minimal user-decision evidence in the project record.

## Next action

State one immediate action, its required inputs, and its expected output.
Explain which uncertainty it reduces and how different outcomes change the next decision.
End with the subfield acceptance decision. Later research actions are proposals until their stage is unlocked.
