# Important Articles for Experimental Work

Start with the accepted question and the evidence needed to answer it. Select articles for the experimental decision they inform. A highly cited discovery, a detailed protocol, and a calibration study can serve different purposes.

Use the researcher's completed work and desired outcome to choose what is still needed. For a concrete proposed experiment, apply [experiment-precedents.md](experiment-precedents.md): list what previous studies performed, attach their verified references, and map consequential proposed steps to actual source locations. A reading list alone does not establish a procedure.

## Select a useful reading route

Include the roles the project actually needs:

| Article role | What the researcher should learn | Where to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Concept or mechanism foundation | Definitions, governing assumptions, and candidate predictions | Theory, model, or explanatory sections |
| Closest experimental precedent | What has already been demonstrated, under which conditions, with what remaining ambiguity | Main results, Methods, key controls, and limitations |
| Method or protocol | What the method measures and how the measurement is implemented | Full Methods, protocol, supplementary information, apparatus or workflow description |
| Calibration or control study | How to detect artifacts and connect a response to the target quantity | Calibration data, controls, reference comparisons, uncertainty analysis |
| Analysis or validation paper | How to process and model data without losing the information needed for the claim | Code, analysis methods, residuals, baseline comparisons, independent validation |
| Contrary or boundary evidence | Where an attractive interpretation or procedure fails | Negative results, condition dependence, replication, or failure analysis |

Give a short reading order based on dependencies, not a fixed number of papers. Explain which decision each article unlocks and what the reader should extract. Verify bibliographic identity and direct links before presenting real article recommendations. Reviews can orient the learner; operational claims should be traced to the primary method or protocol source when possible.

## Experimental paper card

Record the question ID and paper ID, priority reason, verified citation, reading/access status, and exact Methods/SI/figure/code locators. Then extract:

- What was varied, held fixed, measured, and compared.
- Sample or system preparation and operating conditions that affect transfer.
- Which physical or operational quantity the instrument actually reports.
- Calibration, reference materials, blanks, controls, repeat structure, and checks for artifacts.
- The analysis chain, model assumptions, reported uncertainty, and criterion used to interpret outcomes.
- What the reported evidence establishes and what it leaves unresolved.
- Which steps can be adapted, which require local verification, and which essential details are unavailable.

Keep reported procedure separate from your proposed adaptation. Attach source locations to parameter values; preserve units and context. Do not infer missing settings from a plot or pretend an abstract supplies a reproducible procedure. If the full method is inaccessible, classify the article as a conceptual lead and seek a usable primary protocol or documentation.

## Convert reading into a proposed workflow

Map `question -> needed evidence -> method choice -> source-backed procedure -> controls -> analysis -> decision`. Compare the preferred method with a feasible alternative and explain what each can distinguish. Identify prerequisites and the smallest calibration, replication, or feasibility task before a full study when needed.

The resulting workflow is a proposal until the necessary details, equipment access, local applicability, and execution authority are established. Learning a protocol or accepting a review does not mean its experiment has been performed.

During stage 2, keep this a question-feasibility and reading sketch. During stage 3, make the paper cards searchable. During stage 4, synthesize them into the review's experimental guidance using the accepted corpus. A new real experiment is a separate requested activity.
