# What Prior Studies Did and How the Proposed Experiment Relates

For every substantive experimental recommendation, show the relevant prior experiments and attach their references. A bibliography alone does not explain how the procedure was derived. Scale the comparison to the question; do not require an arbitrary number of studies.

## Verify the closest precedents

Search the target measurement and system, equivalent terminology, method/protocol papers, controls, and known failure cases. Inspect the original Methods, supplementary information, protocol, or code needed for operational claims. Record the search boundary and access gaps.

Verify citation identity, version, and direct DOI, publisher, or authoritative repository link. Link the supporting supplement or protocol separately when it contains the actual procedure. A copied citation or a model-generated summary is a lead until checked.

If supplied excerpts lack bibliographic identities, retain their supplied labels and locations in an unverified source ledger and request or retrieve the missing identities. Fictional examples stay labeled fictional. Neither kind belongs in a verified reference list until supported by a real inspected source.

Classify relevance as a direct experimental precedent under comparable conditions, a related method or system requiring adaptation, a calibration/control precedent, an analysis precedent, or a theoretical proposal. A proposed experiment in a paper is not a performed experiment. Never blend conditions from different studies into a procedure that appears to have been validated as a whole.

## Prior-experiment comparison

Use one row per relevant study, or concise paper cards when a wide table is hard to read:

| Study and verified reference | What they actually did | Conditions and controls | Result and limitation | Source locator and access | Relevance to our proposed experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authors/year, title or paper ID, DOI/direct link | System/preparation, setup, intervention, acquisition, and analysis actually reported | Reported values with units, reference/calibration, independent units/repeats when available | Measured or derived result, uncertainty, artifact or scope limit | Methods/SI section, page, figure or code location actually inspected | Direct precedent or explicit analogy; transferable step and remaining mismatch |

Use "not reported in the inspected material" when that is all that is known. Do not turn absence in an abstract into a claim about the complete paper. Preserve distinctions between temperature, timing, dose, geometry, normalization, and calibration where they change applicability.

## Proposed procedure with source traceability

| Proposed step and purpose | Supporting study and exact location | What the source reports | Proposed adaptation and justification | Missing detail or validation | Evidence used to decide whether to proceed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation, calibration, intervention, acquisition, processing, or analysis | Paper ID and inspected locator with reference link | Original conditions, method, and limits | Keep unchanged or explain the change for the intended setup | Unspecified setting, transfer assumption, control, or feasibility estimate | Observable result or check tied to the research question |

Every consequential experimental choice needs a source, a documented local requirement, or an explicit proposal label with its reasoning. This does not mean every routine sentence needs a separate citation. Quantitative settings require their particular source and applicability; do not supply convenient defaults for missing information.

Novel adaptations are allowed when clearly separated from published precedent. Explain the hypothesis behind the change, the uncertainty it introduces, and the smallest validation needed. Agreement between several model answers does not validate the adaptation.

## Attach usable references

Include numbered or author-year citations at the relevant steps and a complete reference list in the same deliverable. Include title, authors, year, venue, identifier and working direct link as verified, plus supplementary/protocol links when used. Supply BibTeX or another reference export when useful or requested.

"Attach references" means make the sources identifiable and reachable. Full-text files are optional and require lawful access and appropriate sharing; do not bundle copyrighted full texts by default. For an unavailable source, state the access limit and what part of the recommendation remains unverified.

## Keep plan, evidence, and execution distinct

The result should make clear what others demonstrated, what the researcher already did, what is now proposed, and what still needs validation. If essential procedure details or applicability evidence are missing, deliver a reading/feasibility plan rather than call it execution-ready.

Within stage 2 this is a bounded question-feasibility sketch. Stage 3 stores the precedent and procedure records in the paper website. Stage 4 synthesizes them using its accepted corpus. Execution of a new experiment remains a separately requested task.
