# Claim and Citation Audit

Use this audit while drafting and before presenting stage 4 for acceptance. Scale detail to the significance of the claim. A central mechanism claim needs more scrutiny than a basic definition with an authoritative source.

## Record the claim's evidence

For each consequential claim, retain:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Claim ID and wording | The exact proposition made by the review. |
| Question ID | The accepted question this claim helps answer. |
| Corpus snapshot | The accepted source set and version. |
| Paper ID and citation | Stable library identity, verified bibliography entry, and source link. |
| Locator | Relevant page, section, theorem, figure, table, or supporting-information location. |
| Access and reading status | What was actually examined, including missing methods or supporting information. |
| Reported evidence | Observation, estimate, derivation, proof, or measured comparison. |
| Interpretation | What the original authors conclude and what this review infers. |
| Conditions and assumptions | Definitions, population/system, measurement or model conditions, uncertainty, and inference requirements. |
| Relation to claim | Supports, qualifies, challenges, or supplies background. |
| Unresolved issue | Missing evidence, incomparable conditions, contradictory results, or limited access. |

Do not let a citation substitute for describing the evidence. A paper can be relevant to the topic while failing to support the particular sentence.

## Audit inference strength

Trace nontrivial claims through observation, processing or estimation, inferred quantity, explanation, and consequence. Identify assumptions at each link. A stronger functional or causal conclusion may require evidence beyond a signal change, association, fit, or prediction score.

Ask whether alternative explanations survive. Check whether controls address those alternatives, whether the required comparison is actually present, and whether observed differences exceed relevant uncertainty. Do not create thresholds absent from the study or appropriate external criteria.

For theoretical claims, verify assumptions and the exact proposition proved. For computational claims, distinguish implementation correctness, evaluation performance, model consequences, and empirical validity. For observational claims, identify whether the study supports association, prediction, or a causal estimand under stated assumptions.

## Audit comparisons and disagreement

Before calling studies contradictory, compare their question, definitions, system or population, conditions, measurements, analysis, and uncertainty. Different operating regimes can produce different answers without contradiction. Conversely, comparable conflicting findings must not be removed merely to produce a smooth narrative.

Identify shared sources of evidence. Preprint/journal versions, reused data, follow-up analyses of one experiment, and overlapping samples can reduce apparent independence. Preserve version relationships and count distinct evidence sources when making a replication or convergence argument.

Do not average unlike quantities or rank methods across incompatible benchmarks. Explain the limiting comparability and retain separate conclusions where necessary.

## Audit experimental guidance

For every recommended experimental route, identify the selected question, the inference it enables, and the accepted library papers that support the method. Check the exact methods, protocol, calibration, controls, and analysis locations rather than inferring procedures from a result figure.

Separate reported source conditions from proposed conditions for a new study. Explain which aspects can plausibly transfer and which require revalidation. Do not turn a source's instrument settings or successful sample into universal defaults. Missing details, inaccessible supporting information, and differences in system or operating range must remain visible.

Check that the reading route gives a reason and an extraction task for each paper, and that its order follows prerequisites. A method paper without evidence for the target inference may still teach technique, but cannot by itself justify the intended scientific conclusion. A paper outside the accepted corpus must pass the stage-3 update gate before it enters the review's evidence base.

## Audit access and citation identity

Check titles, authors, year, venue, and persistent identifiers against an authoritative record. Resolve the intended version and check relevant correction or retraction notices when visible or when a discrepancy warrants verification. Do not replace unavailable evidence with a citation generated from memory.

Reopen original evidence for central, numerical, disputed, or surprising claims. If only an abstract is accessible, restrict the claim to what that abstract reports and disclose the limit. An abstract cannot establish that an unexamined control or analysis is adequate.

Check every in-text reference resolves to a bibliography entry and that the entry points to the intended source. Remove unused entries from the review bibliography unless a separate reading list is explicitly useful and labeled. Preserve all accepted corpus entries in the source library even when the review cites only a subset.

## Audit gap claims

For each proposed open question, identify the nearest existing result and the unresolved difference. Label whether the gap is explicitly acknowledged by primary work or inferred by this review. Bound absence claims to the search scope and cutoff.

Classify the gap accurately: missing evidence, inadequate precision, inconsistent evidence, structural nonidentifiability, an untested setting, a practical limitation, or incomplete corpus coverage. An unfamiliar concept calls for learning; an inaccessible paper calls for an access note or retrieval task. Neither by itself establishes a field-level unknown.

If an audit discovers evidence outside the accepted corpus that could change the review, record the candidate and its consequence. Return the proposed corpus change to stage 3 for acceptance before using a revised source set as the review's basis. If it changes the selected question or subfield, reopen that earlier decision as well.

## Decide readiness for acceptance

The review is ready when its central claims are traceable, bibliography identities are checked, meaningful disagreements and access limits are visible, and the conclusions stay within the selected questions and evidence. A bounded review can be ready despite documented gaps when those gaps do not contradict its presented claims.

If a central claim remains unsupported, narrow it, report it as unresolved, or repair the evidence record before calling the review ready. Mark a review draft as a draft when it still depends on unsettled upstream changes.

Present the concrete version and the remaining limitations to the user. Record acceptance only from the user's actual decision; passing this audit does not supply that decision.
