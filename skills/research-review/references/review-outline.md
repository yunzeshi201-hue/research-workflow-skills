# Review Structure and Deliverables

Use this guide after stage 3 is accepted. Adapt the length and organization to the selected questions; the headings below are functions to fulfill, not a fixed table of contents.

## Establish the source contract

Record the accepted subfield and exclusions, question IDs and wording, website or local preview reference, corpus snapshot ID/version, search cutoff, and access limitations. Include a concise account of how the corpus was assembled and screened. If selection was targeted rather than comprehensive, say so.

Confirm that the corpus is available for inspection. A snapshot should preserve stable paper IDs, verified metadata, inclusion decisions, source links, and reading/access status. Keep any deeper reading performed for this review in a derived evidence record that references the unchanged accepted snapshot.

Select the review form supported by the work. A focused critical narrative review can give a useful, bounded synthesis. Use systematic or scoping labels only when the corresponding methodology has actually been performed and documented. Do not retrofit a procedural label to a hand-selected reading list.

## Explain the problem

Introduce the defined subfield, why the accepted questions matter, and the few concepts needed to evaluate the answers. Explain what a successful answer would change. Avoid opening with unsupported claims about rapid growth or universal importance.

State the review's contribution as a synthesis of evidence and unresolved decisions. It may compare methods, reconcile conditions, expose an inference gap, or clarify the next discriminating question. Its value need not depend on claiming an unprecedented topic.

## Build the main sections around questions

For each selected question, write a section that develops an argument:

1. Define the question and the distinctions needed to answer it.
2. Describe the closest established answer or baseline, with primary evidence.
3. Compare the explanations, approaches, or results that change that answer.
4. Explain what the evidence supports, where it conflicts, and why the conditions matter.
5. State the remaining uncertainty and the consequence for the broader question.

Use paper-level notes as inputs to this synthesis. The finished review should show relationships across studies rather than reproduce catalog entries one after another.

When historical development matters, identify the evidence or assumption that caused an interpretation to change. An earlier date alone does not establish influence, priority, or a causal role in later progress. Support those claims if they are included.

## Compare on meaningful dimensions

Choose dimensions from the question, for example the target quantity, operating conditions, model assumptions, controls, uncertainty, validation setting, or resource constraints. Compare methods directly only when they address sufficiently similar tasks. Describe complementary methods by the different constraints they provide.

A comparison table can use:

| Question | Study or approach | Result under stated conditions | Evidence and locator | Important limitation | Consequence for the question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accepted question ID | Stable paper ID or a defined method family | Preserve definition, units, and scope | Source location and reading status | Assumption, access gap, or unresolved alternative | Supported answer, conflict, or next uncertainty |

Do not fill cells with numerical estimates absent from the sources. A missing measurement should remain missing. A normalized response, fitted parameter, proxy, or benchmark score must retain the definition needed for interpretation.

## Explain the state of the evidence

Use distinct categories where they help:

- **Established within specified conditions:** several appropriate lines of evidence support a bounded answer, with remaining assumptions stated.
- **Supported but conditional:** the evidence favors an answer under a model, calibration, or identification assumption.
- **Disputed:** comparable evidence remains inconsistent after examining definitions and conditions.
- **Unresolved:** the available evidence cannot distinguish the relevant answers.
- **Not assessed:** access or corpus coverage does not permit a judgment.

These are explained judgments, not a hidden numerical score. An answer supported in one setting is not automatically a general principle. Repeated uses of one underlying dataset do not provide independent replication.

## Derive a research agenda

For each proposed next question, record:

- The accepted question it serves and the evidence gap it addresses.
- The closest existing answer and the precise remaining difference.
- Why an answer would matter scientifically or practically.
- The evidence needed to distinguish plausible answers.
- Feasibility assumptions and a small first check.
- What a negative or inconclusive result would change.

Rank only when the available evidence and stated resource assumptions support a meaningful comparison. Explain tradeoffs rather than forcing a fixed number of opportunities or a numerical score. A review can conclude that replication, calibration, better definitions, or a stronger benchmark is more useful than another mechanism proposal.

Keep agenda items as proposed work. The review stage does not authorize new experiments, new collection, or a new autonomous research loop.

## Experimental approaches and practical reading route

When experiments are relevant, connect the selected questions to methods through the decisions each method can support. Explain which observations distinguish plausible answers, what each method measures directly, and which interpretations still depend on models or independent evidence. Compare alternative approaches on discrimination, feasibility, operating conditions, and limitations.

Build a short reading route from the accepted library. Important papers may establish the phenomenon, demonstrate a discriminating experiment, document a protocol, expose an artifact, specify calibration, or justify an analysis method. Select papers for these roles; neither publication venue nor citation count establishes that a paper is a suitable experimental guide.

| Reading step and paper ID | Experimental decision it informs | Exact section, figure, methods, or supporting-information locator | What to extract or demonstrate | Transfer limits and unresolved details |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A source in the accepted corpus, placed by dependency | Choice of method, control, calibration, analysis, or interpretation | Inspected source location and access status | Reconstruct a prediction, compare controls, check a calibration assumption, or reproduce a bounded analysis | Different sample/system, geometry, time or resolution regime, model assumption, or unavailable procedure |

Use this path when it fits: understand the target phenomenon and competing answers; examine a decisive primary study; study the required method and controls; check calibration and analysis; then choose a feasible pilot. Adapt the order to actual dependencies rather than requiring every category or a fixed number of papers.

Summarize procedures at the level supported by the inspected methods. Preserve reported conditions and distinguish them from recommendations for a new system. Parameters absent from the sources or not transferable to the intended setup remain unresolved. An article's result alone does not establish that its method can be reproduced from the information available.

End with a decision path: if the required observable and controls are attainable, propose the smallest informative pilot; if calibration or interpretation is unresolved, address that uncertainty first; if the available method cannot distinguish the answers, compare another observable or narrow the question. State the relevant reading prerequisite for each branch.

If a necessary methods or protocol paper is missing from the accepted corpus, record a proposed stage-3 addition with its relevance. Obtain acceptance of the revised corpus before citing it as part of the review's evidence base. Keep proposed experimental work distinct from executed procedures and results.

## Deliver a traceable package

The minimum useful package is the review, verified references, the source-boundary statement, and a compact audit of consequential claims. The audit may be a separate table or part of working notes when a short review does not justify extra files.

Use stable question and paper IDs to link the argument to the library. Add verified website links when available. If the website is local or inaccessible to the intended reader, provide an agreed portable export or bibliography rather than a broken link.

State which review version is ready for acceptance and summarize material limitations. User acceptance settles that version. Later changes to the question set, corpus, or a central conclusion require the affected part to be reviewed again; small formatting repairs need not reopen unrelated stages.
