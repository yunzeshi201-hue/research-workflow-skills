# Website and Corpus Acceptance

Use the checks that bear on the accepted site behavior. Record results and material verification limits. Completion of a checklist does not substitute for the user's stage decision.

## Content integrity

- Each displayed paper has a stable ID, verified bibliographic identity, and a usable primary source or repository link.
- Duplicates and related versions are linked or reconciled. Counts distinguish papers, versions, evidence items, and reading status.
- Each substantive evidence item maps to an accepted question and an inspected source location.
- Observations, author interpretations, simulations, proposals, and editorial synthesis are visibly distinguishable.
- Conditions, controls or comparisons, units, and evidence limits are retained where they affect the conclusion.
- Method and representation labels reflect their actual role rather than a misleading interface grouping.
- Missing full texts, unresolved metadata conflicts, contradictory findings, and unverified evidence remain visible.
- Experimental-guidance recommendations identify their practical role, reading priority, inspected protocol/methods locator, controls, transfer limits, and missing implementation details; abstract-only records are not presented as complete procedures.
- Figures have an inspected source, correct locator, appropriate attribution, and a documented basis for reuse or a source-link fallback.
- Each key figure explains the authors' intended claim, measured signal, comparison/control, inference, and limits; observed evidence is distinct from interpretation.
- Method/protocol links identify the correct figure panels. Literature images, researcher data, simulations, and explanatory illustrations are visibly distinguished.
- Main-text, supplementary, and figure-inspection statuses are independent; "all methods" is not claimed from a partial reading.

Sample records from every important evidence/access category and any records carrying the main scientific claims. Checking identifiers alone does not check the science. A bounded verification pass must state its coverage rather than claim that every paper was fully reviewed.

## Functional behavior

Exercise the core reading flow with real records:

1. Search a title, author, identifier, or meaningful keyword supported by the implementation.
2. Combine relevant filters, confirm their semantics, and inspect a no-results state.
3. Open a paper detail and return without losing the selected context.
4. Follow a paper or evidence link and confirm it targets the intended source or locator.
5. Export filtered or selected records and confirm their IDs, evidence relations, reading status, and source fields match the display.
6. Reload the site and check that saved state behaves as advertised. For shared persistence, verify an independent session where available.
7. For a multi-method paper, switch between two methods and verify that figures/panels change correctly while the recorded method list remains visible. Check mixed-method figures and combined filters.
8. Inspect a missing-image/source-link-only record, image enlargement, and a broken-image fallback. Confirm that no unrelated image fills an evidence gap.

Where practical-reading guidance is included, test the path from an accepted question through its required evidence and method to the recommended paper and its calibration, control, or analysis support. Confirm that the experimental-guidance and protocol-access filters return the intended records.

Check narrow-screen readability and keyboard operation of the main filters, details, and exports. Verify empty, missing-data, and error states relevant to the implementation. Remove placeholder controls or label unsupported functionality.

For a changing source, record the tested revision. Do not claim a later revision passed tests that were run only on an older one.

## Corpus coverage and review handoff

Create a coverage table by accepted question. Include relevant records, key supporting and contrary evidence, nearest prior work, source access, and consequential missing checks. Explain whether available evidence supports an answer, exposes a disagreement, or only identifies a further reading need.

Coverage sufficiency depends on the planned review claim and scope. A bounded narrative review can be useful with explicit limitations. A systematic or scoping review requires its corresponding documented methods, and exhaustive coverage needs supporting search and screening evidence. Do not use paper count as the acceptance rule.

Freeze and retain:

- The accepted subfield and question versions.
- The website source or deployed version and its delivery location.
- The corpus snapshot identifier, creation date, structured export, and bibliography export when available.
- The search/screening record, cutoff, scope, exclusions, and source-access limitations.
- The per-question evidence coverage and remaining contradictions.
- Figure/panel evidence mappings, asset/rights status, and main-text versus supplementary inspection gaps.
- The checks performed, failed or unavailable checks, and unresolved functional limitations.

Verify that the website and exported snapshot contain the same records and mappings. Record integrity hashes or equivalent version identifiers when useful. The review must cite the same accepted snapshot rather than a mutable unversioned list.

## Acceptance checkpoint

Present the working artifact and a short settlement summary: what the website does, what corpus it covers, what is verified, what remains limited, and what review that evidence can support.

Ask whether the user accepts this site and corpus version for the review stage or wants specific revisions. Explain that the checkpoint implements the requested sequential workflow. Acceptance of appearance alone is not acceptance of corpus scope unless that was explicitly part of the decision presented.

Record the user's explicit acceptance, the referenced artifact versions, and any accepted limitations. Do not manufacture a quote or mark acceptance from silence. A publication success, automated test pass, or agent's confidence is not the user's acceptance.

If changes affect accepted questions or scope, identify which earlier decision needs settlement and mark dependent artifacts stale. Preserve previous artifacts and work that remains valid. If only stage-3 defects remain, repair those and return the updated artifact to the same checkpoint.

After acceptance, hand the snapshot to the review stage. The acceptance does not by itself authorize additional public sharing, scheduled refreshes, or unrelated experiments.
