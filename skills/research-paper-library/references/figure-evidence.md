# Figures That Explain Claims and Evidence

Use original, inspected article figures to answer: what were the authors trying to establish, what did they measure, what signal supports the claim, and what remains unresolved? A figure is evidence to interpret, not decorative proof.

## Reading flow

Connect the accepted question to a method, relevant paper, matching figure/panel, observation, control or comparison, supported inference, and remaining limitation.

When a reader selects a measurement, show a concise guide before the paper results: what it measures directly, which signal matters, what can be inferred under stated assumptions, and which controls or complementary methods are needed. Keep method family, detection channel, experimental protocol, and analysis representation separate.

Keep the main paper list compact: question, key claim, reading status, recorded methods, and a relevant preview when an inspected reusable image is available. Expand into readable figures, panel explanations, conditions, controls, and source links. Do not pack a full review into every table row.

## Figure and panel evidence card

| Field | Required explanation |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The authors' research question and the claim they intended to establish |
| Source | Verified paper identity and version, article or supplement, figure/panel number, direct source link, inspected locator |
| Method | What was measured or calculated, system/preparation, intervention, relevant conditions, and protocol |
| Read the plot | Axes, units, normalization, legend, and the observable feature or comparison to examine |
| Evidence | Reported change, contrast, trend, distribution, bound, or derived quantity; uncertainty and controls where supplied |
| Inference | How the observation bears on the claim and the assumptions connecting them |
| Limits | What the panel supports, plausible alternatives, missing controls, and what it cannot establish alone |
| Practical lesson | What to look for in a comparable experiment, transfer limits, and the next discriminating check |
| Provenance | Attribution, license/permission basis, asset or source-link status, inspection status, and any crop or annotation |

Keep authors' interpretation separate from the website's assessment. Say what is supported under the reported conditions; do not promote an intended claim to demonstrated proof. Preserve contrary, null, and ambiguous findings. Distinguish measured data, derived quantities, simulations, proposals, and explanatory illustrations.

If a claim depends on several panels or studies, show each contribution and whether the conditions are comparable. Multiple methods can share a confounder; their number alone does not establish independent confirmation.

## Match the selected method to its evidence

Map figures and panels explicitly to paper, question, claim/evidence, method, and protocol IDs. These are many-to-many relations. Do not select figures just by filename, list position, or a broad topic tag.

For a multi-method paper, switching the selected method must show its corresponding figures and identify relevant panels of mixed-method figures. Keep the paper's recorded method list visible. Call that list complete only when the relevant article and supplements were checked; otherwise label it "methods recorded" and disclose the inspection gap.

Declare combined-filter semantics. With several methods selected, identify which method each panel supports. A paper can match a method while its image is unavailable; show that gap rather than substitute another method's figure.

Separate these states: source uninspected, figure identified but image unavailable, image inspected, reuse basis unresolved, source link only, and image included. None implies a method was absent or a scientific result was negative.

## Attach inspected images

Inspect the original figure, caption, relevant text, and needed Methods/SI before explaining scientific or operational details. Verify the displayed asset's paper and version. A search thumbnail or model-generated description is insufficient.

Attach the original or a labeled crop when reuse is supported. Preserve axes, units, legends, error bars, panel labels, and essential context. Label annotations and retain a link to the original. Do not generate, redraw, smooth, or reconstruct experimental traces to replace unavailable evidence. A separately labeled conceptual illustration never counts as an original evidence figure.

If reuse is unresolved, provide the exact source/figure link and an original concise explanation with a visible image gap. Do not bundle full copyrighted papers by default or copy long captions. Provide meaningful alternative text, readable enlargement, and a broken-image fallback retaining the source and explanation. An image is not raw numeric data and does not justify unreported precision.

Distinguish published figures from user-supplied measurements. A literature image must not appear to be a result obtained by the researcher. Private uploads and local analysis are not automatically cleared for a public website; retain their provenance and authorized audience.

## Reading status, export, and verification

Track main-article access/reading, supplementary access/reading, and figure/panel inspection independently. "Main text read" does not mean supplementary methods and figures were checked. Show the relevant status beside the claim.

Export figure/panel-to-method-to-claim relations with sources, locators, conditions, interpretation limits, asset status, and attribution. An asset manifest distinguishes included images, links, and missing items. Figure count alone does not measure evidence coverage.

Use [site-acceptance.md](site-acceptance.md) to verify method switching, panel matching, attribution, and missing-image behavior before presenting the website and corpus for acceptance. The review uses that accepted evidence version.

## Generalize reference websites

When the user names reference sites, inspect their reading flow, method categories, evidence-card structure, and usability problems. Transfer reusable design decisions. Never copy their topic, paper list, figures, claims, project names, site links, or private notes into public skills. Any distributed example must be clearly fictional and independent of those projects.
