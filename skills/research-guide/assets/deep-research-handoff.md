# Research Consultation Brief

The coordinator fills this brief from available evidence and sends it as the initial message of the authorized same-project consultation. The user does not need to find or paste it. Keep a completed brief outside the public skill package. Select the product and model through actual tools; this text cannot select or verify them.

## Context to fill before dispatch

- Consultation and question IDs; brief/evidence revision.
- Active stage and accepted scope, question, and corpus versions, where applicable.
- Completed work, user-reported observations, inspected evidence, interpretations, and plans, labeled separately.
- Desired outcome and the specific decision the answer would change.
- Available resources, constraints, unknowns, and competing explanations or approaches.
- Inspected sources, Methods/SI/figure locators, accessible evidence excerpts, contrary evidence, and access gaps.
- Requested product/model and the actual configured selection when known; never infer identity from the answer's prose.

If there is not enough context to state a useful question, gather the missing account in the main chat before dispatch. Do not invent research, fill unknown settings with defaults, or create an empty consultation merely to ask for the same intake again.

## Request to send with the completed context

Analyze the bounded question above within the stated stage. Compare the plausible explanations or approaches, state assumptions, and identify evidence that would distinguish them. Explain the strongest supported answer, the strongest remaining alternative, and what would change your assessment.

For experimental recommendations, show what prior studies actually did. Use inspected primary papers, Methods, supplementary information, protocols, or code. Include a comparison with verified references and direct source links, system/preparation, setup, reported conditions, controls, analysis, observed results, limitations, and exact source locations. Distinguish direct precedents from related-system analogies and new proposals.

Map consequential proposed steps to their supporting sources. Separate reported settings from adaptations, explain the adaptations, and mark missing parameters. Attach the reference list. If no published precedent supports a proposed step, say so and explain the hypothesis and validation needed. A citation or an abstract cannot fill missing procedure details.

Return the smallest useful next action, its completion criterion, and what informative, null, or inconclusive outcomes would imply. If access or procedure information is insufficient, return a provisional feasibility plan and exact retrieval needs, rather than an execution-ready protocol.

State any consequential missing facts as clarification needs in this chat. Do not create further chats, message other chats, edit shared project artifacts, publish material, operate equipment, or advance a research stage. The coordinator will retrieve your final answer, verify it, and decide what to incorporate; your response does not accept a stage on the user's behalf.

Keep project-specific information private. Use only evidence actually supplied or accessible to you. Cite the relevant question and evidence revision in the final answer, then give recommendations, source support, limitations, and proposed changes to the active work.
